## What to steal (8 entries)

### 1) Childish Gambino — “This Is America” (2018), dir. Hiro Murai, DP Larkin Seiple
- **Source:** Matt Mulcahey, “DP Larkin Seiple Breaks Down Every Shot from Childish Gambino’s ‘This Is America’,” *Filmmaker Magazine*, May 31, 2018. https://filmmakermagazine.com/105396-dp-larkin-seiple-breaks-down-every-shot-from-childish-gambinos-this-is-america/
- **Also:** Logan Baker / Ernie Gilbert interview, *PremiumBeat*, June 8, 2018. https://www.premiumbeat.com/blog/interview-editor-this-is-america/
- **Steal list**
  - **Color / tone:** Vision3 500T; organic film color over digital “corpse” skin; slight overexposure for richer highlights; green fluorescent spill kept as texture.
  - **Cut rhythm:** Six Steadicam “chapters”; few hard cuts — whip-pans / tilt-to-wall as **structural resets**, not beat spam.
  - **Motif return:** Foreground dance + artist held while background chaos loops (fire, cops, looting) — same warehouse, escalating threat.
  - **Verse vs chorus grammar:** Long takes = hypnotic verse/bridge endurance; dramatic reset when music/section demands a new act.
  - **Instrument→image:** Performance/body owns frame; percussion “hits” land as **events in the take** (gun, choir fall) more than as cut-per-kick.
- **Light Show translation**
  - Prefer **continuity morph** inside a world; hard-cut only at section gates (pre→chorus, drop).
  - Keep one **center subject** (lead beams / lyric) while pads/fog carry background weather.
  - Escalate motif strength on chorus repeats without teleporting to a new planet.

### 2) The Weeknd — “Blinding Lights” (2019 video / 2020 awards cycle), dir. Anton Tammi, DP Oliver Millar
- **Source:** Matthew Allard ACS, “How The Weeknd’s ‘Blinding Lights’ music video was shot,” *Newsshooter*, May 24, 2020. https://www.newsshooter.com/2020/05/24/how-the-weeknds-blinding-lights-music-video-was-shot/
- **Also:** Adam William Wilson (production designer) interview, *Interiors* journal. https://www.intjournal.com/the-weeknd
- **Steal list**
  - **Color:** Persistent **red** motif via practicals + existing urban red light (locations chosen for red availability); anamorphic + diffusion = soft night bloom.
  - **Cut / camera:** Driving POV, steadicam, Russian-arm chase light — motion as chorus energy; empty club = verse/bridge oasis.
  - **Motif return:** After Hours universe — red, night highway, wounded run — same brand world across clips.
  - **Verse vs chorus:** Club/interior = intimate lyric pocket; highway speed + neon streaks = open anthem.
  - **Instrument→image:** Title-literal “blinding” lights as active staging (low shutter → light as streaks); pads/atmosphere = fogged night air.
- **Light Show translation**
  - Prioritize `empty_highway` + `rainy_city` / `futuristic_city` with **warm-red practical blooms** on chorus.
  - Verse: slow push-in, low hats density; chorus: horizon kick + scale vignette + lead beams.
  - One white-out max on drop/gate — then hold neon readable (no spam).

### 3) Beyoncé — “Formation” (2016), dir. Melina Matsoukas
- **Source:** Wesley Morris interview with Melina Matsoukas, *The New York Times*, Dec. 28, 2016. https://www.nytimes.com/2016/12/28/arts/music/melina-matsoukas-beyonce-formation-interview.html
- **Also (motif/color rhetoric, secondary):** Union Penumbra, “The Rhetoric of Beyoncé’s Formation.” https://unionpenumbra.org/article/the-rhetoric-of-beyonces-formation/
- **Steal list**
  - **Color:** Deliberate costume/light symbolism (black mourning / power; red–white spiritual/power pairings discussed in secondary criticism); tableau staging.
  - **Cut rhythm:** Montage of **locations as chapters** — each returns as a motif island (porch, pool, sinking car, police line).
  - **Motif return:** Recurring formations, hats, water, “Stop shooting us” — callbacks strengthen meaning on each hit.
  - **Verse vs chorus grammar:** Verse/narrative tableaux vs chorus performance blocks (pool / porch / formation lines).
  - **Instrument→image:** Hook/chorus = synchronized group geometry; quieter phrases = single-figure tableau.
- **Light Show translation**
  - Plant 2–4 **visual motifs** in verse; on chorus.repeat enlarge the same motifs (scale / brightness / type size).
  - Hard-cut between tableau “acts,” morph within an act.
  - Lead + lyric own WHAT; kick/snare only punctuate formation flashes.

### 4) *Mad Max: Fury Road* (2015), dir. George Miller, ed. Margaret Sixel, DP John Seale
- **Source:** Vashi Nedomansky, “The Editing of MAD MAX: Fury Road,” *VashiVisuals*, May 30, 2015. https://vashivisuals.com/the-editing-of-mad-max-fury-road/
- **Also:** Steve Hullfish, Art of the Cut with Margaret Sixel, *ProVideo Coalition*, March 13, 2016. https://www.provideocoalition.com/art-cut-margaret-sixel-editor-mad-max-fury-road/
- **Steal list**
  - **Color:** Desert chroma + storm teal/orange contrast (public craft discourse emphasizes clarity over chaos).
  - **Cut rhythm:** Extremely fast cutting **with** center framing / eye-trace so the brain never hunts; rising rhythms then poetic rests (Vuvalini).
  - **Motif return:** War Rig, chain, center “crosshair” subject — same spatial grammar across set pieces.
  - **Verse vs chorus:** Quiet middle = welcome “verse”; chase = chorus/drop plateau — musicality of sequences (“stay on the wave”).
  - **Instrument→image:** Doof Warrior / drums as on-screen rhythm engines; editor talks rising rhythms like music without needing temp score early.
- **Light Show translation**
  - **Center the hit:** kick → horizon punch always in the same LED “crosshair”; snare → mid flash same locus.
  - Fast role events OK **if** framing stable; chaos cinema (random shake + random flash) forbidden.
  - After drop, allow a **breakdown rest** (pads sky fog) so later chorus hits still land.

### 5) *Blade Runner 2049* (2017), dir. Denis Villeneuve, DP Roger Deakins
- **Source:** Bill Desowitz, “‘Blade Runner 2049’: The Most Difficult Craft Challenges,” *IndieWire*, Nov. 24, 2017. https://www.indiewire.com/awards/industry/blade-runner-2049-roger-deakins-denis-villeneuve-1201900768/
- **Also:** Matt Grobar, Deakins interview, *Deadline*, Feb. 26, 2018. https://deadline.com/2018/02/blade-runner-2049-roger-deakins-oscars-cinematography-interview-1202280774/
- **Steal list**
  - **Color script:** Cold silver/white–blue smog exterior → Wallace **artificial sunlight / yellow** → Vegas **red–ochre dust**; yellow as journey thread (brick-road metaphor).
  - **Cut / camera:** Pools of light in large black; geometric moving light feels “alive”; atmosphere (mist/rain/snow) as volume.
  - **Motif return:** Yellow door / flower → red Vegas with yellow edges still lingering.
  - **Verse vs chorus:** Cold open = intro/verse; golden interior = elevated bridge; red Vegas = emotional chorus/apex.
  - **Instrument→image:** Sustained pads ≈ fog volume; lead ≈ moving caustic beams; silence ≈ large black negative space.
- **Light Show translation**
  - Build a **section color temperature arc** (cool verse → warm pre → saturated chorus) instead of random hue churn.
  - `futuristic_city` / `rainy_city` / `white_void` / `red_void` as scripted world pack, not shuffle.
  - Pads → sky fog intensity; lead → center beams; silence → hold black pools (don’t invent sparkle).

### 6) Radiohead — “Karma Police” (1997), dir. Jonathan Glazer
- **Source:** Ryan Dombal, “This Is What You Get: An Oral History of Radiohead’s ‘Karma Police’ Video,” *Pitchfork*, March 21, 2017. https://pitchfork.com/features/ok-computer-at-20/10036-this-is-what-you-get-an-oral-history-of-radioheads-karma-police-video/
- **Steal list**
  - **Color:** Blood-red backseat; night road; fire as terminal palette flip.
  - **Cut / camera:** Mostly driver POV — viewer complicit; simple sentence-story held for the whole clip.
  - **Motif return:** Chase grammar reverses (hunter↔hunted); fire pursues as final motif escalation.
  - **Verse vs chorus:** Patient stalk = verses; ignition/fire = chorus/bridge climax.
  - **Instrument→image:** Arrangement tension → camera pressure; climax = elemental takeover (fire), not cut spam.
- **Light Show translation**
  - One strong POV: slow push-in / dutch only when `build` rises; hard flip on drop (`harsh` glitch/ash or red_void bloom).
  - Motif callback: return the same road/void with inverted energy (verse intimidation → chorus pursuit of light).

### 7) Cibo Matto — “Sugar Water” (1996), dir. Michel Gondry
- **Source:** Andrew Clayman, “Cibo Matto’s ‘Sugar Water’: the mind-bending symmetry of a song and its video,” *Far Out Magazine*, 30 Sep 2025. https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/sugar-water-most-mind-bending-music-video/
- **Steal list**
  - **Color / form:** Split-screen symmetry as the visual “arrangement.”
  - **Cut rhythm:** Almost no classical cutting — **structure is choreography + reverse time**.
  - **Motif return:** Palindrome: mirrored actions meet, swap sides — the callback *is* the form.
  - **Verse vs chorus:** Video form mirrors song’s symmetrical pre/chorus architecture (“la-la” center).
  - **Instrument→image:** Song structure dictates image structure more than drum hits.
- **Light Show translation**
  - Use **mirrored motif returns** (same sky fog / beam pattern reversed or swapped layers) on chorus.repeat.
  - Prefer morph/symmetry over rapid hard cuts when arrangement is circular/palindromic.

### 8) Published “language of music-video clips” (editors + large-scale study)
- **Source:** arXiv preprint (ISMIR-adjacent): “Is there a ‘language of music-video clips’? A qualitative and quantitative study,” arXiv:2108.00970. https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2108.00970  
  (Interviews: composer Jack Bartman; director Alexandre Courtès; editor Maxime Pozzi — cites Firework cut density by section; Adele off-beat climax cuts.)
- **Steal list**
  - **Cut rhythm:** Cuts accelerate near climaxes; bridges often slower/poetic; off-beat cuts as surprise at emotional peaks.
  - **Verse vs chorus:** Functional segments (verse/chorus/bridge) consciously guide editing density.
  - **Instrument→image (published habits):** Cuts often co-occur with downbeats/beats (genre-dependent); anticipation (cut slightly before downbeat) used for climax energy; sync strength varies by genre (Pop segment boundaries; R&B/Reggaeton downbeats).
  - **Motif / narration:** Editing as narration — not every hit needs a cut; some climaxes want off-grid accents.
- **Light Show translation**
  - Map: **kick** → grounded beat-synced horizon; **snare** → mid flash (can anticipate by a few frames on pre fills); **hats** → dense sparks only when arrangement densifies; **pads** → long fog during bridges; **harsh** → off-beat glitch at drop.
  - Raise cut/event rate in chorus/drop; lower in verse/breakdown per Pozzi’s chorus-vs-bridge intuition.

## Patterns table — visual grammar by section

| Section | Scale / world | Cut / transition | Color / light | Dominant roles → LED verbs | Camera |
|--------|---------------|------------------|---------------|----------------------------|--------|
| **Verse** | Intimate→human; plant motifs; negative space | Morph / long take; rare hard cut | Cooler or restrained palette; practical pools | `lead` beams+lyric, `pads` sky fog, light `hats` sparks, sparse `kick` | Slow drift / gentle push-in; no dutch spam |
| **Pre** | Same world, pressure | Anticipation cuts; build without climax | Warmth rising; contrast up | Rising `hats`/`snare`, `bass` vignette pressure, `build` | Push-in; optional dutch as tension only |
| **Chorus** | Open / cinematic→epic; motifs **bigger** | Higher event rate; still center-framed | Saturated motif color (red neon, gold sun, etc.) | `kick` ground+shake, `bass` scale, `snare` mid flash, `lead` huge type, brighter `pads`/`hats` | Stable crosshair + controlled shake on kick |
| **Drop** | One gate shock | Hard cut / white-out **once** | Extreme flash then settle | `kick`+`harsh`(+`bass` surge); then commit | One shake burst; do not hold chaos |
| **Breakdown** | Rest / silk; lyric or pure music | Slow morph; poetic long holds | Desaturate or fog-heavy | `pads` fog, sparse `kick`, optional ash fade | Static / float; restore readability |

Distilled from: Murai/Seiple long-take chapters; Weeknd red night open; Matsoukas motif islands; Miller/Sixel center-frame speed; Deakins color script; Glazer POV escalation; Gondry structural symmetry; arXiv/Pozzi section cut density.
