## What to steal — characters

### 1) Childish Gambino — “This Is America” (2018), dir. Hiro Murai / DP Larkin Seiple
- **Source:** Mulcahey, *Filmmaker Magazine*, May 31, 2018 — https://filmmakermagazine.com/105396-dp-larkin-seiple-breaks-down-every-shot-from-childish-gambinos-this-is-america/
- **Steal:** One **center body** owns the frame for long stretches; background chaos escalates *around* the performer (cops, fire, looting) without stealing the crosshair.
- **Light Show:** Always keep a readable silhouette / figure at center-thirds; pads/fog/weather = BG weather; kick/snare = events that happen *to* the world, not a cut away from the person.

### 2) Beyoncé — “Formation” (2016), dir. Melina Matsoukas
- **Source:** NYT / Matsoukas coverage cited in companion notes; Formation as tableau chapters.
- **Steal:** **Single-figure tableau** for intimate lines; **group geometry / formation** on hooks; costume + architecture as character.
- **Light Show:** Verse = 1 figure (walker, kneeler, looker); chorus = multiply silhouettes or widen formation; never empty worlds when characters are ON.

### 3) The Weeknd — “Blinding Lights” (2019/20), DP Oliver Millar
- **Source:** Allard, *Newsshooter*, May 24, 2020 — https://www.newsshooter.com/2020/05/24/how-the-weeknds-blinding-lights-music-video-was-shot/
- **Steal:** Character *moves through* the world (club → highway → street); camera chases the body; red practicals paint the person and the environment together.
- **Light Show:** Figure is not a sticker on a postcard — path along highway / wet street; lighting washes hit figure + world as one system.

### 4) Mad Max: Fury Road (2015) — center-frame action
- **Source:** VashiVisuals / Art of the Cut discussions in companion notes.
- **Steal:** Eye-trace stays on the human (or vehicle-as-character) even at high cut rates.
- **Light Show:** On phone LED, **never lose the figure** during kick shake or drop gate — scale the world behind them.

## What to steal — environmental / scenic typography

### 5) Saul Bass — *North by Northwest* (1959) titles
- **Source:** Art of the Title — https://www.artofthetitle.com/title/north-by-northwest/
- **Steal:** **Situational type** — text aligns to building grid / perspective; type becomes architecture, not a lower-third caption.
- **Light Show:** Prefer `integrated` layout: type sits on horizon, wet street reflection, neon sign plane, or skyline — match world perspective; avoid floating karaoke bar.

### 6) Saul & Elaine Bass — *West Side Story* (1961) end titles
- **Source:** Art of the Title — https://www.artofthetitle.com/title/west-side-story/
- **Steal:** Credits discovered as **graffiti / signs on walls** — words are part of the city’s skin.
- **Light Show:** Hook words can live on billboard, tunnel wall, cathedral glass, highway sign, or rain/fog as luminous phrase — diegetic first, overlay last.

### 7) Kyle Cooper — *Se7en* (1995) titles
- **Source:** Art of the Title — https://www.artofthetitle.com/title/se7en/
- **Steal:** Type *characterizes* the world (hand-etched psyche) — texture and aggression match music/harsh roles.
- **Light Show:** On `harsh` / drop gates, type may jitter/etch/ash; on pads/silk, type is clean architectural light. Never purple karaoke highlight bars (already banned in typography system comments).

### 8) Diegetic intertitle practice (general)
- **Source:** Ian Warn, “The Rise of the Diegetic Intertitle” — https://ianwarn.net/the-rise-of-the-diegetic-intertitle/
- **Steal:** Integrate text into the plate so it feels *in* the story world; avoid opaque wall between viewer and fantasy (especially on small screens).
- **Light Show:** Scrim only when needed for phone contrast; prefer world-lit type (neon, shaft, reflection) over permanent black plate karaoke.

## Anti-patterns (CEO: not karaoke)

| Karaoke / lyric-video smell | Directed head-movie instead |
|-----------------------------|-----------------------------|
| Centered caption every line, same Y | Type changes plane: street / sky / wall / glass by section |
| Highlight bar chasing words | Soft glow / weight from `lead` + VocalEstimate only |
| Empty postcard world + text on top | Figure(s) stage the lyric meaning |
| New random background each phrase | One world; characters + type evolve inside it |
| Type fights drop white-out | Hold type or etch at gate; restore readable figure first |

## Patterns table — character + type by section

| Section | Characters | Typography |
|---------|------------|------------|
| Intro | Reveal silhouette; establish scale | `integrated` — quiet words as signage / fog |
| Verse | 1 figure walks / stands / looks; plant motif props | `cinematic` blocks but **anchored** to world plane |
| Pre | Figure leans into camera / path narrows | Type tightens / anticipates (smaller → larger) |
| Chorus | Formation widen OR figure heroic scale | `title` huge — still world-lit (neon/shaft), not floating karaoke |
| Drop | Figure holds or vanishes 1 beat; world shocks | Etch/harsh type OR silence type for kick/harsh ownership |
| Breakdown | Figure small / kneeling / distant | Soft integrated phrases or instrument-owned no-type |
| Outro | Figure dissolves into world / stars | Type fades into environment (rain→stars, sign→void) |
